Why Cleanup Is Different in Houston

Here's something most people don't realize. Houston ranks number one among the 25 largest U.S. cities for the highest percentage of homes with pest infestations. Nearly 15 percent of homes here deal with mold, rodents, cockroaches, or all three.

Now picture a hoarder house in that environment.

The city averages about 75 percent humidity year round. On summer mornings, it regularly hits 90 percent. Mold starts growing when indoor humidity goes above 60 percent. In a home where stacked newspapers, cardboard boxes, and old clothes block the air vents and keep the AC from circulating, mold can take hold within a day.

A crew that worked a home in Sharpstown found the owner had stacked newspapers from floor to ceiling in every room for over a decade. When they moved the first pile, the wall behind it was black with mold. Hoarding cleanup in Houston isn't just hauling stuff to the curb — the city's climate demands a much more thorough approach.

Hoarding Disorder Is Real

Hoarding disorder affects about 2 to 6 percent of the U.S. population. It was classified as its own diagnosis in the DSM-5 in 2013. Addressing it properly requires more than someone with a truck and some trash bags — it takes real professionals who understand compulsive hoarding and treat people with respect.

What to Expect

What a Professional Cleanout Actually Looks Like

A good property cleanout follows a clear process. It's not a crew showing up and throwing everything in a dumpster.

1

Assessment Using the Clutter-Hoarding Scale

It starts with someone coming to the home and looking at the full picture. How many rooms are affected? Are there signs of mold, pest waste, or structural damage? Is there anything hazardous? This assessment determines the scope — it's the difference between a crew working for a few days and a project that takes weeks and may require contractors for repairs.

2

Sorting, Not Just Trashing

This is where it gets personal. A good team doesn't just dump everything. They work with the homeowner to sort items: keep, donate, recycle, trash. In Houston, usable furniture can go to the Houston Furniture Bank. Building materials and home goods can go to the Habitat for Humanity ReStore locations. Clothing and household items go to Goodwill or Salvation Army. A good decluttering service will know these options and build donation runs into the cleanup process rather than sending everything to the landfill.

3

Deep Cleaning and Sanitizing

After the clutter removal is done, the real cleaning starts. In Houston's climate, that almost always involves dealing with mold. The combination of year-round humidity and poor airflow in packed homes means mold colonies grow behind furniture that hasn't moved in years, under belongings stacked in bathrooms and kitchens, and in closets with no ventilation. The job may also involve removing rodent droppings (crews wear PPE), handling hazardous materials like old paint, chemicals, expired medications, and contaminated waste.

4

Repairs and Restoration

In some homes, the damage goes beyond cleaning. Floors sag. Plumbing has failed. Walls have water damage. Older homes may have aging infrastructure that can't handle years of neglect on top of existing wear. We offer repairs or can connect homeowners with contractors who handle restoration work after the cleanout.

Hidden Treasures

Professional crews have found valuable things buried in these homes: cash hidden in old coat pockets, jewelry mixed in with junk mail, important documents stuffed in grocery bags. One cleanup professional shared a story about a Houston job where they found a 170-page handwritten life story in a torn paper bag that the client almost threw away. Crews in the Houston area have also reported recovering large amounts of cash. Those kinds of finds are why a crew that treats every item like garbage isn't the right crew for this work.

Hoarding happens everywhere — in mansions in River Oaks and in apartments in Greenspoint, in newer subdivisions in Katy and in mid-century homes in Oak Forest. But certain areas see more calls than others.

The Heights, Montrose, Eastwood & the East End

▼

Older inner-loop neighborhoods with homes built starting in 1891 and many over a hundred years old. Poor ventilation, outdated plumbing, and limited storage space all make hoarding problems worse faster in these homes.

Understanding

The Emotional Side Nobody Talks About

Here's what nobody prepares you for. The hardest part isn't the mess. It's the feelings.

Hoarding disorder is a mental health condition, not a character flaw. The DSM-5 classifies it under Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders. Even when someone is ready for a cleanout, forcing the process without sensitivity can cause a relapse. The stuff comes right back. The most effective cleanouts happen when the mental health component is addressed alongside the physical work.

Houston's Hoarding Ordinance

Houston's hoarding ordinance applies to multi-unit buildings — apartments, condos, and townhomes. Single-family homes are not included.

For single-family homes, Houston code enforcement can still cite property owners for trash accumulation (fines from $200 to $2,000), conditions that attract rodents, dangerous building conditions, or inoperable appliances stored outside.

Protect Yourself

Questions to Ask a Hoarding Cleanup Company

Not every company is equipped for this work. Here's what to look for.

🛡️

Are You Insured and Bonded?

Any legitimate company carries general liability insurance at minimum. If they're doing biohazard or mold work, they should carry additional coverage. Ask for proof. If they hesitate, walk away.

📋

Do You Have Experience With Hoarding Specifically?

Junk removal and hoarding cleanup are different jobs. A junk hauling crew that usually clears out garages may not know how to handle the emotional side, the biohazard risks, or the sorting process that a hoarder home requires.

🎓

What Certifications Does Your Crew Have?

Look for training from the Institute for Challenging Disorganization (ICD) or NAPO. For biohazard work, OSHA bloodborne pathogen training is standard. For mold, Texas requires a specific remediation license.

🏠

Will You Do a Walkthrough Before Quoting?

Any company that gives you a price without seeing the home is guessing. A proper assessment means someone comes to the property, evaluates the severity level, checks for hazards, and gives you a written estimate. If they want to quote over the phone based on square footage alone, that's a red flag.

Absolutely. Our crews arrive in unmarked vehicles, and we never discuss client situations with neighbors or third parties. Your privacy is our priority throughout the entire process.

What's included in the price?

▼
Ask whether the quote covers hauling fees and dump charges, dumpster rental, cleaning after the clutter is removed, and mold or pest treatment. We provide a clear, written breakdown so you know exactly what you're getting. No hidden fees, no surprises.

Can the homeowner be involved in sorting?

▼
Yes, and we encourage it when possible. We work with the homeowner or their representative to sort items into categories: keep, donate, recycle, and discard. We never throw anything away without approval unless it poses a health or safety hazard.

Do you handle mold remediation?

▼
Yes. Mold is extremely common in Houston hoarding situations. Texas requires a mold remediation license for this work, and our team is fully licensed. For severe situations, we may recommend a certified mold inspector for testing before and after cleanup.

What if the person with hoarding disorder won't accept help?

▼
