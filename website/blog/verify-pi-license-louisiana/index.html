How to Verify a Private Investigator’s License in Louisiana

Hiring a private investigator is a significant decision. You are trusting someone with sensitive personal matters — a suspected cheating spouse, a custody dispute, a fraud claim — and you are paying for evidence that may eventually appear in a Louisiana court. Before you hand over a retainer, the single most important step you can take is confirming that the investigator holds a current, active license issued by the Louisiana State Board of Private Investigator Examiners (LSBPIE). This article walks you through exactly how to do that, what a valid license contains, and what warning signs should send you looking elsewhere.

The Louisiana State Board of Private Investigator Examiners (LSBPIE)

Louisiana regulates the private investigation industry under Louisiana Revised Statutes Title 37, Chapter 23 (R.S. 37:3501 et seq.). The LSBPIE is the state agency charged with licensing, oversight, and discipline of all private investigators practicing in Louisiana. The board was established to protect consumers and to maintain minimum professional standards across the industry.

Any person who — for compensation — conducts investigations, surveillance, background checks, or fact-finding services on behalf of another person or entity in Louisiana must hold an active LSBPIE license. This requirement applies to both individual investigators and the agencies that employ them. There are two primary license categories: the Private Investigator License (for individual practitioners) and the Private Investigation Agency License (for firms).

What Does the License Lookup Show?

The LSBPIE maintains a public license verification database on its official website at lsbpie.com. To search, you simply enter the investigator’s or agency’s name, license number, or city. The results will display:

  • Full legal name of the licensee or agency

  • License number and type (individual PI or agency)

  • Current license status (active, inactive, expired, or revoked)

  • Original issue date and expiration date

  • Parish or city of business address

  • Any formal disciplinary actions on record

If you are hiring a PI firm in Metairie, verify both the agency’s license and the license of the individual investigator who will be handling your case. Both must be active.

Age and Background

The applicant must be at least 18 years old and a United States citizen or lawful permanent resident. Applicants submit to a full FBI fingerprint-based background check. Any felony conviction, any conviction involving moral turpitude, or any disqualifying criminal history will result in a license denial. A thorough background check is not merely a formality — it is a legal prerequisite.

Experience Requirement

Louisiana requires a minimum of three years (approximately 3,000 hours) of documented full-time investigative experience before an individual may sit for the licensing examination. This experience must be verifiable, typically through employment records or an affidavit from a supervising licensed PI. The intent is to ensure that licensed investigators have meaningful fieldwork behind them before they take on independent clients.

Examination and Insurance

Applicants must pass a written examination administered by the LSBPIE covering Louisiana law, ethics, investigative procedures, and evidence handling. Licensed agencies are also required to carry general liability insurance and, in many cases, errors and omissions (E&O) coverage.

Penalties for Unlicensed PI Work in Louisiana

Practicing as a private investigator in Louisiana without a valid LSBPIE license is a criminal offense under R.S. 37:3517. A first offense is classified as a misdemeanor, punishable by a fine of up to $500 and up to six months in jail. Repeat or aggravated violations can result in felony-level prosecution. The LSBPIE also has authority to pursue civil injunctions to stop unlicensed operators.

Beyond the criminal exposure to the unlicensed PI, there is a direct consequence for you as the client: evidence gathered by an unlicensed investigator is far more vulnerable to being excluded in Louisiana civil and criminal proceedings. A family law attorney opposing your custody case, for example, will immediately move to suppress any evidence collected by an investigator who cannot demonstrate licensure at the time of the investigation. This is one of the strongest practical reasons to verify credentials before signing any contract. You can learn more about how evidence admissibility works in our article on Louisiana surveillance laws for private investigators.

Red Flags When Hiring a Private Investigator

A license lookup is the foundation, but it is not the only check you should perform. The following behaviors are warning signs that an investigator or agency may not be operating ethically or legally:

Inability or Refusal to Provide a License Number

Any legitimate Louisiana PI will immediately and voluntarily provide their LSBPIE license number. If an investigator hedges, changes the subject, or outright refuses, treat this as a serious red flag. Verify the number they give you against the LSBPIE database before proceeding.

Guarantees of Specific Outcomes

A licensed, ethical investigator cannot guarantee what evidence will be found — because investigations follow facts, not client wishes. Any PI who promises they “will find evidence of infidelity” or “will prove your ex is an unfit parent” is misrepresenting the nature of investigative work. Outcomes cannot be pre-determined.

Requests to Perform Clearly Illegal Activities

Some unlicensed operators — and a small number of rogue licensed ones — will offer to obtain phone records through pretexting, access password-protected accounts, or install covert tracking software on a device without proper legal authority. These activities are illegal under Louisiana and federal law, and any evidence obtained through such methods will be inadmissible and could expose both the investigator and the client to criminal liability. A trustworthy PI will clearly explain the legal limits of GPS tracking and other surveillance tools.

No Written Contract

Every professional PI engagement should begin with a written service agreement that specifies the scope of work, hourly or flat rates, retainer terms, deliverables, and confidentiality obligations. Verbal-only arrangements protect no one. Our guide on how to hire a private investigator in Metairie covers what a solid contract should include.

Unusually Low Rates

Licensed PIs in Louisiana have real overhead: insurance, licensing fees, equipment, and professional training. If a quoted rate is dramatically below market — say, $25–$40 per hour when the going rate in the New Orleans metro area is $75–$150 — that should prompt questions. Unlicensed operators often compete on price because they have none of the legitimate costs of running a properly licensed business. Our article on private investigator costs in New Orleans explains what fair market rates look like.

Why Licensing Matters for Evidence Admissibility

Louisiana courts, particularly in Jefferson Parish Family Court and the civil district courts of Orleans Parish, scrutinize the provenance of investigative evidence closely. A licensed PI operating within the bounds of Louisiana law produces reports, photographs, video, and surveillance logs that carry significant weight in legal proceedings. The investigator can be deposed or called as a witness, and their LSBPIE credentials will be reviewed by opposing counsel.

By contrast, evidence gathered by an unlicensed individual — even if the underlying activity was technically legal — raises immediate authenticity and chain-of-custody concerns. Opposing counsel will challenge the evidence, and judges are often receptive to those challenges. In a hotly contested divorce or child custody dispute in Jefferson Parish, the difference between licensed and unlicensed investigative evidence can be the difference between winning and losing your case.

If you are working with a family law or civil litigation attorney, they will almost certainly insist on a licensed PI. Summary: Your Pre-Hire Verification Checklist

Before you sign with any private investigator in Louisiana, run through these steps:

  • Ask the investigator for their LSBPIE license number and the agency license number.

  • Look both up at lsbpie.com — confirm the status shows “active” and the expiration date is current.

  • Check whether any disciplinary actions appear on the record.

  • Request a written service agreement before paying any retainer.

  • Ask how they handle evidence documentation, chain of custody, and report delivery.

  • Confirm they carry liability insurance.

  • Ask about their experience with cases similar to yours — infidelity investigations, skip tracing, background checks, or workplace investigations each require different skill sets.

Taking fifteen minutes to verify a license can save you thousands of dollars and protect the integrity of your case.
