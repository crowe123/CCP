Skip Tracing in Metairie, Louisiana: How It Works and When You Need It

When someone cannot be found through ordinary means — they have moved without leaving a forwarding address, disconnected their phone, or actively avoided contact — a process called skip tracing becomes necessary. Licensed private investigators in Metairie and Jefferson Parish use skip tracing regularly for attorneys, creditors, families, and process servers who need to locate individuals who have become difficult or impossible to reach. This article explains exactly what skip tracing is, who uses it, how it works step by step, and what you can realistically expect from the process.

What Is Skip Tracing?

The term "skip tracing" originated in the debt collection industry — "skip" referring to someone who has skipped town without paying what they owe, and "tracing" describing the investigative process of following their trail. Today the term applies broadly to any effort to locate a person whose current whereabouts are unknown.

Skip tracing is distinct from simple internet searching. While anyone can type a name into a search engine, a licensed PI conducting a skip trace has access to professional-grade databases, public records aggregators, and cross-referencing tools that are not available to the general public. The combination of these resources with trained investigative methodology is what makes professional skip tracing effective where personal searches fail.

Skip tracing services in the Metairie area are requested by a wide range of clients:

  • Attorneys who need to serve process on a defendant or locate a witness before a deposition or trial.   • Insurance companies and their counsel who need to locate a claimant, witness, or defendant in connection with a claim or lawsuit.

The Skip Tracing Process: Step by Step

Step 1: Information Gathering

Before any database search begins, the investigator collects everything the client already knows about the subject. This includes the subject's full legal name, date of birth, Social Security number (if available), last known address, phone number, email address, vehicle information, employer, and names of known associates, relatives, or former neighbors. The more starting information provided, the faster and more reliably a skip trace can be completed.

Step 2: Professional Database Searches

Licensed PIs have subscriptions to databases that aggregate data from credit bureaus, utility companies, motor vehicle departments, court systems, and dozens of other sources. These are not the same services available through consumer websites — they are professional tools that require licensure to access and provide far more comprehensive and current data than public search engines.

In Louisiana, these databases can surface address history across all sixty-four parishes, vehicle registrations, professional license records, and business entity filings maintained by the Louisiana Secretary of State's office.

Step 3: Public Records Review

Louisiana public records provide a rich investigative resource. The Jefferson Parish Clerk of Court maintains civil and criminal court records that can indicate an address used by a party during litigation. Property records filed with the Jefferson Parish Assessor's Office show real estate ownership. Louisiana Secretary of State business records list registered agents and officers for LLCs and corporations — sometimes revealing the subject's current address through a business filing. Civil suit filings, judgment records, and UCC filings all provide additional data points.

Step 4: Social Media and Digital Footprint Analysis

A subject who has deliberately hidden their physical address may not have been equally careful online. Social media profiles, photo geotagging, check-ins, public posts referencing locations, and connections to other identifiable people can all provide location intelligence. This type of investigation — known as OSINT, or open source intelligence — is entirely lawful when conducted from publicly available sources. Learn more about what a PI can find through OSINT techniques.

Step 5: Cross-Referencing and Confirmation

Individual data points are rarely conclusive on their own. The investigator cross-references data from multiple sources to identify consistent patterns — for example, an address appearing in both utility records and a court filing from the past six months is far more reliable than an address that appears only once in an older database entry. Once a probable current address is identified, field confirmation may be conducted to verify that the subject is actually present at that location before a process server is dispatched.

What Information Helps a PI Start Faster

The quality of your starting information directly affects how quickly a skip trace can be completed. The most useful data points are:

  • Full legal name and any known aliases or name changes
  • Date of birth
  • Social Security number (if available — dramatically speeds database searches)
  • Last known physical address and the approximate date they were last confirmed there
  • Vehicle make, model, and license plate
  • Known relatives' names and locations
  • Former employer's name and address

Do not be discouraged if you have only a name and a general location. Experienced investigators in the New Orleans metro can often make progress with minimal starting data — it simply takes more time and cross-referencing.

Skip Trace vs. Missing Person Investigation: What's the Difference?

These two terms are sometimes used interchangeably, but they describe different situations. A skip trace assumes the subject is alive and has simply moved or become difficult to contact — the goal is to find a current address or phone number. A missing person investigation in Jefferson Parish typically involves greater urgency, the possibility that the person may be in danger, and a different investigative methodology that may include coordination with law enforcement, interviews with family members, and canvassing of last known locations.

If someone has been missing for more than 24 hours, particularly a vulnerable adult or a minor, contact Jefferson Parish Sheriff's Office or the New Orleans Police Department first. A licensed PI can supplement law enforcement efforts, but should not replace them in a true missing-person emergency.
