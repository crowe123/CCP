What a Licensed Investigator Can Do in Louisiana

This is where most competitor pages go vague. Here is the straight version.

A licensed PI in Louisiana can legally conduct covert surveillance in any public place. They can photograph and film a subject (the person being investigated) on public streets, in parking lots, outside restaurants, or in any space where there is no reasonable expectation of privacy. Court-admissible evidence gathered this way meets Louisiana evidentiary standards.

Louisiana is also a one-party consent state for audio recording. That means a person who is part of a conversation can legally record it without telling the other party. A licensed investigator operating in Louisiana works within that framework.

What a PI cannot do is equally important to understand. They cannot enter private property without permission. They cannot tap a phone line or plant a listening device. They cannot impersonate law enforcement, which is a federal crime regardless of the state. They cannot use pretexting (false identity tactics) to obtain phone records or financial records. Both are specifically illegal under federal law. Any evidence gathered through those methods is not just inadmissible. It can sink the entire case and expose the client to legal liability.

Licensed investigators in Louisiana are governed by Louisiana Revised Statutes 37:3500-3525 and regulated by the Louisiana State Board of Private Investigator Examiners (LSBPIE). They are bound by strict professional conduct rules including confidentiality, honesty, and avoidance of conflicts of interest.

Digital Investigations and Open-Source Intelligence

A growing number of people looking for investigative help want to know specifically about digital work. Can an investigator look at someone's social media? Can they trace online activity? The answer is yes, within the bounds of what is publicly available.

Licensed investigators use open-source intelligence (OSINT) to gather information from publicly available digital sources. That includes public profiles on Facebook, Instagram, X, TikTok, and LinkedIn. If a subject posts publicly, an investigator can document it. A claimant who says they are injured but posts gym photos. A person who says they are in Metairie but keeps tagging locations in Houston. These contradictions surface in public posts and they are fair game in court.

Other OSINT techniques include reverse image searches to verify identity, username enumeration to find the same person across multiple platforms under different handles, precision search queries to surface buried public information, and public records research through court databases, property records, and professional licensing boards. Investigators working at this level also use specialized databases like TLO, IRB Search, and Tracers to pull information that is not available through standard public searches.

What a PI cannot do digitally: hack into private accounts, access private messages, obtain phone records through deception, or access financial accounts. That is illegal regardless of the justification. What they can do legally is often more than enough. People reveal a surprising amount about themselves in public posts.

Investigation Services

Services a Private Investigator Handles

A full-service investigator in this area covers more ground than most people expect.

Surveillance

Covert surveillance is the core of most domestic and insurance cases. The investigator observes a subject, logs every detail (date, time, weather conditions, location, vehicle, activity) and backs it all up with time-stamped photos and video. That written record is called a surveillance log, and it is the foundation of any court-admissible case file.

Surveillance comes in two forms. Stationary surveillance means watching a fixed location like a residence or workplace. Mobile surveillance means following a subject through traffic, which requires a different skill set entirely. Experienced investigators use techniques like paralleling (following on a parallel street to avoid detection) and positioning a trigger (a second investigator stationed near the subject's location to alert the team when the subject moves). Getting "burned," meaning the subject notices the surveillance, can compromise the entire case. A local investigator who knows Jefferson Parish roads has a significant advantage during mobile work. Running surveillance on Causeway Boulevard during rush hour is a completely different operation than following someone at 11 p.m. on a quiet residential street in Bucktown.

Background Checks and Due Diligence

These go far deeper than a Google search. An investigator accesses court records, financial filings, employment history, and criminal databases that the general public does not have access to. People use these before hiring a contractor, entering a business partnership, or getting married. A Metairie business owner once discovered through a due diligence investigation that the contractor they were about to sign with had three active civil fraud judgments in Orleans Parish. That check cost a fraction of what a bad contract would have.

Skip Tracing and Locates

Skip tracing combines database research, OSINT, public records, and field canvassing to find people who do not want to be found. Canvassing means physically visiting locations and talking to neighbors, coworkers, and contacts who may know where a subject has gone. It is old-fashioned fieldwork and it still works. Investigators run locates when someone needs to find a missing family member, serve legal documents to an evasive individual, or track down a debtor who skipped town.

Process Serving

Process serving is the delivery of legal documents to someone who needs to receive them and sometimes actively avoids it. Licensed investigators in the Metairie area handle difficult service of process throughout Jefferson Parish and Orleans Parish.

Asset Investigations

Asset investigations are used in divorce proceedings, debt recovery, and civil litigation. If someone claims to have nothing while clearly living well, a discreet investigation can uncover hidden bank accounts, unreported property, undisclosed vehicles, and concealed income.

One thing worth knowing before you call: results are not always what you expect. Experienced investigators will tell you that a meaningful percentage of infidelity cases come back clean. The spouse is not cheating. The suspicious behavior has an explanation. When that happens, a clean report can bring genuine peace of mind. No evidence is still a result.

The investigator needs to blend into the area, document the subject cleanly, and avoid being noticed. A typical infidelity surveillance runs two to seven days of active field observation, depending on the subject's routine and schedule predictability.

During the case, a professional investigator keeps the client updated, usually after each observation session. The client is never left waiting in the dark for a week. When the case closes, the client receives a full written report with time-stamped evidence. If the evidence is needed for divorce proceedings, it is prepared in a format that meets Louisiana court standards.

Child Custody Investigations in Jefferson Parish

When children are involved, the stakes change. Jefferson Parish family court judges need specific, documented evidence, not a feeling, not a suspicion, not something a third party mentioned on the phone.

A licensed investigator handling a child custody case builds that documented record systematically. The target behaviors typically include whether the other parent is following the custody order, whether the children are in a safe environment, whether the other parent is exposing the kids to dangerous individuals or situations, and whether appropriate supervision is being provided.

The evidence that moves a custody case in court is time-stamped surveillance footage paired with a detailed written observation log showing what was observed, when, where, and under what conditions. An investigator's report showing a parent repeatedly violating the custody schedule, backed by dates, times, and video, carries real weight with a judge. That evidence also passes through a proper chain of custody, a documented record of who collected it, when it was collected, how it was stored, and how it was transferred, to ensure nothing can be challenged as altered or fabricated.

Both Catalyst Private Investigations and A&M Investigations, two of the established firms in this area, list child custody cases among their primary services, which reflects how frequently this type of domestic investigation comes up in Jefferson Parish.

Insurance Fraud and Workers' Compensation Surveillance

Louisiana keeps investigators busy with insurance-related work. Disability claims, workers' compensation fraud, and staged accident investigations are a significant part of the caseload in this state.

Here is how a workers' comp surveillance case typically works. A claimant files an injury claim. The insurance company hires an investigator to conduct mobile surveillance on the claimant, often starting on days with scheduled medical appointments, because that is when the insurance company knows the person will leave the house. The investigator logs everything: the time the claimant exits the residence, what they are doing, what their physical condition appears to be, and what activities contradict their claimed limitations. All of it goes into a surveillance log that can be submitted as court-admissible evidence.

Insurance fraud investigation requires patience, detailed documentation, and an investigator who understands how Louisiana workers' compensation law interacts with the evidence standards insurance carriers require. Cutting corners on documentation can render an otherwise solid case unusable.

What the Final Report Contains

When an investigator closes a case, the client receives a professional written report. A proper report includes the date, start time, and end time of each observation session. It documents weather and lighting conditions, the specific location where the subject was observed, a physical description of the subject and any vehicle, and a factual, objective log of the subject's activities during the observation period. Time-stamped photographs and video footage are attached. The report closes with the investigator's name, signature, and Louisiana license number.

This report is designed to go directly to an attorney. In Louisiana, a licensed investigator can also provide a sworn affidavit attesting to their observations and can testify in court as a fact witness if the case goes to trial. Always ask before hiring: will you provide a written report, and can you testify in court if needed? A professional answers yes to both without hesitation.

What It Costs to Hire a Private Investigator

Most people are reluctant to ask about cost upfront. But it is one of the first things you should discuss.

In the greater New Orleans metro area, most investigators charge between $75 and $200 per hour for standard surveillance work. The rate depends on the investigator's experience, the complexity of the case, and whether the work requires a single operative or a team. Highly specialized cases or multi-vehicle mobile surveillance operations can run higher.

Beyond the hourly rate, most investigators require a retainer before work begins, typically between $500 and $5,000 depending on the case type. The retainer is drawn down as work is performed. If the case runs longer than expected, the retainer may need to be replenished. Ask upfront whether any unused balance is refundable.

There are also costs that do not always show up in the base rate: travel and mileage to a surveillance location, database access fees during a locate or background check, charges for report preparation and video compilation, and additional fees if the investigator needs to appear in court or give a deposition. A professional will walk through the full fee structure clearly before any agreement is signed.

If someone gives you a vague answer about pricing or refuses to put the agreement in writing, that is a red flag.

Before You Call: What to Have Ready

The first consultation is free and confidential. Most investigators in the Metairie area offer a free consultation with no obligation, so there is no reason to wait until you have every detail sorted. You do not need to have everything figured out. But having a few things written down makes the conversation faster and the investigation more efficient.

For infidelity or domestic surveillance: the subject's full name and date of birth if known, home and work address, vehicle make, model, color, and license plate, a recent photo, the days and times the suspicious behavior usually happens, and any locations you think they frequent.

For a child custody case: the custody order details and any violations you have observed, the other parent's address and vehicle description, and names of anyone the children have mentioned who concerns you.

For a background check or due diligence investigation: the full legal name, date of birth, and last known address of the person being checked.

For a missing person locate: last known address and contact information, a physical description and recent photo, the last confirmed time and location the person was seen, and names of anyone they might have contacted.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire

Not every investigator is the same. Before putting money down, ask these questions directly.

Are you licensed in Louisiana? Licensing is required under Louisiana R.S. 37:3500-3525, administered by the LSBPIE. Ask for their license number, then go to lsbpie.com and verify it yourself. If they cannot provide a number or they are not listed, walk away.

Do you carry insurance and are you bonded? Louisiana law does not specifically require this as a public mandate, but any professional investigator should carry both. It protects the client if something goes wrong during fieldwork.

Do you specialize in this type of case? An investigator who mostly handles corporate due diligence is a very different practitioner from one who runs domestic surveillance and infidelity cases daily. Match the investigator to the case.

What is your fee structure and what does the retainer cover? Get the full breakdown in writing before you sign. Ask specifically what counts toward the retainer and what gets billed separately: travel, database access, report preparation, court time.

Will you provide a written report and can you testify in court if needed? Any professional answers yes to both.

How often will you communicate during the case? A good investigator provides regular updates, typically after each observation session or when something significant develops. You should never feel ignored.
