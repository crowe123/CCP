Most people hire a private investigator only once or twice in their lives — often during one of their most stressful moments. Whether you need surveillance on a suspected cheating spouse, a thorough background check before a major business decision, help locating a missing family member, or evidence for a custody proceeding, the process of finding and hiring the right licensed PI in Metairie doesn't have to be overwhelming. This step-by-step guide walks you through every stage, from identifying your objective to receiving a final report.



## Step 1: Define Your Objective Clearly Before You Call




Before you pick up the phone, take ten minutes to write down exactly what you need to know and why. Private investigators bill by the hour or on a flat-fee basis; clear objectives prevent scope creep and wasted retainer funds. Ask yourself: What specific question do I need answered? What does success look like — a confirmed address, documented evidence, a written report, testimony in court? What is my timeline? What is my budget?




A PI who receives a vague brief ("I want to know what my spouse is doing") will spend billable hours clarifying scope. A PI who receives a focused objective ("I need surveillance documentation of my spouse's activities on Tuesday and Thursday evenings over the next three weeks, for use in a divorce proceeding in Jefferson Parish") can provide an accurate estimate and build a targeted plan. Reviewing our article on [what to expect during a marital investigation](../articles/cheating-spouse-investigation-what-to-expect.html) or our guide to [child custody investigations in Jefferson Parish](../articles/child-custody-investigation-jefferson-parish.html) can help you refine your thinking if your situation involves a relationship or family law matter.



## Step 2: Verify the PI's LSBPIE License




Louisiana law requires private investigators to be licensed by the Louisiana State Board of Private Investigator Examiners (LSBPIE). Operating as an unlicensed PI is a criminal offense under Louisiana Revised Statute 37:3501 et seq. Any investigator you hire in Metairie or Jefferson Parish must hold a valid LSBPIE license. Our dedicated article on [how to verify a PI's license in Louisiana](../articles/verify-pi-license-louisiana.html) provides the exact steps to confirm a license is current and in good standing through the LSBPIE's public online database. Never hire a PI who cannot provide their license number or who becomes evasive when you ask for it.



## Step 3: Prepare for the Consultation




Most licensed PIs in Metairie offer a free initial consultation — by phone or in person. Come prepared with the following information about the subject of the investigation:





- Full legal name and any known aliases or maiden names


- Date of birth (if known)


- Current and recent past addresses


- Vehicle description (make, model, color, plate number if available)


- Social media handles, email addresses, or phone numbers


- Known places of employment or frequent locations


- A clear timeline of the events that led to your inquiry

## Step 4: Ask the Right Questions




The consultation is your opportunity to evaluate the investigator's competence, communication style, and fit for your case. Effective questions include:





- **How many cases like mine have you handled?** Experience with your specific case type — infidelity, workers' comp fraud, missing persons — matters more than general experience.


- **How do you bill?** Understand whether the engagement is hourly, flat-fee, or retainer-based. Get the hourly rate, any minimum commitments, and how expenses (mileage, database fees) are billed.


- **What is a realistic timeframe for this investigation?** A good PI gives you an honest estimate, not an optimistic one to close the sale.


- **What deliverables will I receive?** Will you get a written report? Photographs? Video? An affidavit suitable for court? Know exactly what you are paying for.


- **Who will actually conduct the investigation?** Some firms use subcontractors. Know whether the person you are meeting is the person who will be in the field.


- **Are you familiar with Louisiana law on surveillance and recording?** A PI unfamiliar with Louisiana's [one-party consent recording laws](../articles/louisiana-one-party-consent-recording.html) or [Louisiana surveillance statutes](../articles/surveillance-laws-louisiana-pi.html) is a liability.

## Step 5: Understand the Contract and Retainer Agreement




A licensed, professional PI will provide a written services agreement before any work begins. Read this contract carefully. It should specify: the scope of the investigation, the billing structure and retainer amount, how and when you will receive updates, how unused retainer funds are handled if the investigation concludes early, a confidentiality agreement protecting your information, and the form the final deliverable will take. If a PI offers to start work without a written agreement, treat that as a red flag.




Most Metairie PIs require an initial retainer of $500–$2,500, depending on the complexity of the case. Hourly rates typically run $75–$150 per hour. Our complete guide to [PI costs in the New Orleans area](../articles/private-investigator-cost-new-orleans.html) provides detailed benchmarks across case types.



## Step 6: Know What to Expect During the Investigation




Once the retainer is paid and the agreement is signed, the investigation begins. Expect the following during the active phase:




**Communication frequency:** A professional PI provides regular updates — typically a brief check-in every few days, or immediately when a significant development occurs. You should never have to wonder what is happening with your case. Establish communication preferences (phone, text, encrypted email) at the outset.




**What you will hear:** Factual updates — the subject was observed at a specific location, a database search returned a particular address, a witness agreed to speak. Your PI should be factual and measured, not inflammatory or speculative.

**What you won't hear:** You will not receive real-time play-by-play during active surveillance — a PI cannot be safely conducting surveillance and texting you simultaneously. Updates come at the conclusion of a surveillance session or at end of day.




**Scope changes:** If the investigation reveals information that suggests a different approach — a new location to surveil, a new associate to research — your PI should discuss this with you before expanding scope and incurring additional cost. Never allow scope expansion without your explicit authorization.



## Step 7: Receive and Use the Final Report




At the conclusion of the investigation, your PI delivers a written report containing a factual narrative of all activity, an activity log with timestamps, authenticated photographs or video evidence, copies of documents retrieved from public records, and any witness statements taken. This report is your property.




If the report will be used in legal proceedings — divorce, custody, civil litigation — share it with your attorney before taking any action. A PI report is factual evidence, not legal advice. Your attorney determines how it is used, disclosed, or submitted. Our article on [how Louisiana attorneys work with private investigators](../articles/attorney-private-investigator-louisiana.html) explains how this handoff works in practice.



## What Makes a Good PI in Metairie




The best licensed investigators in the Jefferson Parish area share several qualities: they hold a current, unencumbered LSBPIE license; they have documented experience with cases similar to yours; they communicate clearly and honestly — including about what they cannot accomplish; they produce professional written reports; and they operate within the law at all times. A PI willing to cut legal corners — illegally accessing phone records, trespassing to conduct surveillance, recording conversations without consent in a state requiring all-party consent — creates evidence that may be suppressed in court and exposes you to civil and criminal liability.



## Red Flags to Avoid




Be alert to investigators who: cannot produce a valid LSBPIE license number on request; demand 100% of their fee upfront before any work has begun; guarantee a specific outcome ("I will find proof your spouse is cheating"); cannot explain their billing structure clearly; are unwilling to provide a written contract; make promises about accessing private communications, sealed court records, or protected financial data without legal process; or have no verifiable professional history.



## Confidentiality




Everything you share with a licensed PI — your identity, your situation, the subject of the investigation — is held in strict confidence. A professional PI will not discuss your case with third parties, will not disclose findings to anyone other than you (or your attorney, with your authorization), and will maintain secure storage of case files and recordings. Establish confidentiality expectations explicitly in your services agreement.
