Businesses in Metairie, Kenner, and across Jefferson Parish face a wide range of internal threats — from inventory theft and time card fraud to workers' compensation abuse and the theft of proprietary information. When a company suspects misconduct but lacks the internal resources or legal authority to investigate properly, a licensed private investigator provides the objective, documented findings needed to support HR decisions, disciplinary action, and litigation. This article explains how workplace investigations work in Louisiana, what types of misconduct a PI can address, and how to engage an investigator confidentially and effectively.



## Types of Workplace Investigations



### Employee Theft and Inventory Shrinkage



Retail establishments, warehouses, restaurants, and construction companies in the Metairie corridor frequently experience inventory losses that internal audits cannot fully explain. A PI can conduct covert surveillance of stockrooms and loading docks, identify patterns in loss timing, review access records, and document suspected employees in the act of taking inventory. Physical theft evidence, when documented properly, is admissible in civil recovery actions and criminal referrals to JPSO.



### Workers' Compensation Fraud



Louisiana workers' compensation fraud costs employers and insurers hundreds of millions of dollars annually. A claimant who reports a debilitating back injury but is observed mowing their lawn, lifting heavy objects, or working a second job is committing fraud under Louisiana Revised Statute 23:1208. A licensed PI can conduct lawful field surveillance of claimants at their homes, in public spaces, and at any secondary employment locations, producing timestamped video and photographic evidence that documents the discrepancy between claimed and actual physical ability. Our article on [insurance fraud investigations in Louisiana](../articles/insurance-fraud-investigation-louisiana.html) covers this area in detail.



### Time Clock and Attendance Fraud



Buddy punching — where one employee clocks in for another — and falsified time records cost businesses directly and expose them to payroll liability. A PI can document arrival and departure patterns through surveillance, cross-reference actual presence with clock-in records, and identify whether multiple employees are participating in a coordinated scheme.



### Substance Abuse in the Workplace



Louisiana employers in safety-sensitive industries — construction, transportation, oil and gas services — have particularly strong compliance obligations around substance use. A PI can document observable behavior that suggests impairment during work hours, identify patterns of behavior consistent with substance dependency, and support HR in building a documented record prior to a termination or referral to an employee assistance program.



### Harassment and Misconduct Investigations



When an internal HR investigation into harassment, hostile work environment, or policy violation reaches a dead end — or when a key witness won't speak to company personnel — a neutral third-party PI can conduct interviews, take recorded statements (subject to Louisiana's [one-party consent recording laws](../articles/louisiana-one-party-consent-recording.html)), and compile findings in a format that supports HR decision-making without exposing the company to claims of biased process.

### Corporate Espionage and Trade Secret Theft



The Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016 provides federal civil remedies for companies whose confidential business information is stolen by departing employees or competitors. A Louisiana PI can conduct an investigation to identify how trade secrets were accessed, whether proprietary data was copied or transmitted, and who may be responsible — building a factual record that supports both a TRO (temporary restraining order) and federal civil litigation.

## How PIs Work with HR and Legal Counsel




The most effective workplace investigations involve close coordination between the PI, the company's HR department, and outside legal counsel. Attorneys direct the investigative scope to ensure findings are collected in a manner that preserves privilege where applicable, minimizes civil liability, and produces evidence in a format usable in litigation or arbitration. HR provides internal records, access logs, and documentation of prior disciplinary history that the PI uses as a baseline. This three-party structure is standard in formal workplace investigations in Louisiana, particularly when termination, criminal referral, or civil action is anticipated.




For attorneys working on the legal side of these cases, our article on [how Louisiana attorneys use private investigators](../articles/attorney-private-investigator-louisiana.html) describes how PI findings integrate into the litigation process.



## Covert vs. Overt Investigation Methods




A covert investigation is one the subject does not know is occurring. Covert surveillance, undercover operations, and discreet database research fall into this category. Covert methods are appropriate when alerting the subject would cause destruction of evidence, flight, or the termination of ongoing misconduct before documentation is complete.




An overt investigation is disclosed to relevant parties — typically after the initial covert phase has established a factual basis. Overt methods include formally scheduled interviews, company-authorized records reviews, and announced surveillance of company property. Many workplace investigations use both phases sequentially.



## Louisiana At-Will Employment and Investigation Context




Louisiana is an at-will employment state under Louisiana Revised Statute 23:251, meaning an employer may terminate an employee for any reason not prohibited by law. However, a well-documented PI investigation creates a factual record that insulates the company against wrongful termination claims, unemployment compensation disputes, and EEOC complaints. Documentation of observed misconduct prior to termination demonstrates that the employment decision was based on performance and conduct — not pretextual.



## Surveillance of Workers' Compensation Claimants




Louisiana courts have consistently upheld the right of employers and insurers to conduct field surveillance of workers' compensation claimants in public spaces. Under Louisiana surveillance law — and consistent with the guidelines in our article on [surveillance laws applicable to Louisiana PIs](../articles/surveillance-laws-louisiana-pi.html) — a licensed investigator may photograph or video a claimant anywhere there is no reasonable expectation of privacy: public streets, parking lots, open yards visible from public roads, and commercial establishments. This evidence, when properly authenticated, is admissible in workers' comp proceedings before the Louisiana Office of Workers' Compensation.

In some workplace investigations, a PI or a vetted operative may be placed in a role within the business — as a customer, a vendor, or even a temporary employee — to observe ongoing misconduct firsthand. Undercover operations in Louisiana must be carefully structured to avoid entrapment (a legal defense to criminal prosecution), coercion, or activities that violate Louisiana wiretapping law. Any undercover recording of conversations must comply with Louisiana's one-party consent standard. These operations are almost always conducted under attorney direction.



## Documentation Standards for Termination and Litigation




A workplace investigation report is only as valuable as the documentation supporting it. A professional PI produces a written report that includes: a statement of facts (what was observed and when), a chronological activity log with timestamps, authenticated photographs and/or video, chain-of-custody documentation for any physical evidence, and a summary of findings without legal conclusions. Legal conclusions are reserved for the attorneys. This format is designed to withstand scrutiny in court, before the Louisiana Department of Labor, or in an EEOC administrative proceeding.



## Digital Forensics




When a workplace investigation involves suspected data theft, email fraud, or unauthorized system access, a licensed PI works with certified digital forensics specialists. PI firms do not typically conduct internal forensic examinations themselves — a certified computer forensic examiner (often a Certified Forensic Computer Examiner, or CFCE) handles device imaging, metadata analysis, and file recovery under an evidence-preservation protocol. The PI coordinates this work and integrates forensic findings into the broader investigation report.

## Engaging a PI Confidentially




Confidentiality is essential in workplace investigations. Contact between a business and its PI should be handled through outside legal counsel whenever possible to protect the communication under attorney-client privilege. When contacting a PI directly, limit internal knowledge of the investigation to the smallest possible group — typically the CEO or owner, CFO, and general counsel. Leaks can compromise covert operations and give a subject time to destroy evidence or coach witnesses.
